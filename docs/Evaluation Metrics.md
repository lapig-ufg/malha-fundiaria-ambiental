# 07. Evaluation Metrics of Land Tenure Meshes

This stage shows the methodological structure applied in the comparative evaluation of geometric and topological integrity between the spatial databases Land Tenure Mesh - LAPIG and Cartas da Terra (iGPP). In this analysis, the land tenure void areas of the Cartas da Terra (iGPP) land tenure mesh were not accounted for.

## Metrics used

01. **Coverage Area:** Determines the total mapped territorial extension by each database (usually calculated in hectares or square kilometers). It allows validating the completeness of the dataset at national or regional level, serving as an indicator of under-dimensioning or massive area overlap.
02. **Zero Polygons (Tiny Geometries):** Identifies polygons with null areas, collapsed geometries, or irrelevant microscopic areas (noises generated by processing errors, poorly executed intersection calculations, or coordinate entry typing issues). In production environments of spatial databases (PostGIS), records of this type generate unnecessary processing bottlenecks.
03. **Bad Polygons (Slivers):** Slivers are "needle" polygons, extremely long and thin. They almost always occur due to failures of topological snapping between neighbor property boundaries, generating empty gaps or small strips of double overlap. Measuring the quantity of slivers is the primary thermometer to evaluate the level of topological dirtiness of a mesh. In this case, polygons with a circularity index and area below 0.12 and 1000m² respectively were considered slivers.
04. **Average Circularity Index:** Evaluates the shape factor of polygons (usually applying the Polsby-Popper formula). The index varies from $0$ to $1$, where values close to $1$ represent perfect circles or very compact shapes, and values close to $0$ represent highly branched, linear, or twisted geometries. In land tenure meshes, an excessively low circularity in thousands of records is usually a strong indicator of cartographic distortions or corrupted geometries.
